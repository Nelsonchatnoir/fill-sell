You already sell, most likely on one main site, and you have tried adding a second one. Two things tend to happen, often both: the duplicated listings eat time you do not have, and one day an item sells twice. This article describes a way of selling the same item on four platforms that still holds with fifty items in stock — by hand, or with a tool doing the data entry. We have already covered [what cross-listing is and how to choose a tool](/blog/cross-listing-vinted-leboncoin); this one is about method.

Two of the four platforms, Leboncoin and Beebs, are French. If you sell in France, they matter as much as the two you already know.

## Every extra platform costs you three times

Before the method, the honest arithmetic. One more platform is not one more listing: it is one listing to create, one inbox to watch, and one listing to remove on the day of the sale. The third cost is the one people forget, and it is the one that causes double sales.

Hence a simple rule: do not list everywhere by default. List where the item has a real chance.

- **Adult clothing and shoes**: Vinted first. Leboncoin second if the piece is worth more than a few tens of euros.
- **Children's clothing and gear**: Vinted and Beebs, whose audience is exactly that.
- **Bulky, fragile, or to be handed over in person**: Leboncoin, and often Leboncoin alone.
- **Sought-after pieces, collectibles, electronics, or a buyer who may be outside France**: eBay, on top of the rest.

A €12 pair of jeans on four platforms will cost you more time than it brings back. A €90 branded coat will not.

## One source sheet, then one listing per site

Re-typing is what makes people give up. The answer is to never write *on* a platform: write a neutral, complete sheet first, outside any form, then adapt it.

The source sheet holds everything a site might ask for: brand, model, size, colour, material, condition with the flaws named, flat measurements, what is included, what you paid, your target price. Photos are taken once, for every site: the first on a neutral background, then details, then flaws. A site that caps the number of photos will take the first ones — order them accordingly.

Then, each platform has its own codes, and a listing pasted as-is shows:

- **Vinted**: short title; brand and size sit in dedicated fields that do the work. Brief description, the exact condition, the measurements.
- **Leboncoin**: the title is what the buyer types in the search box. Longer description; say whether hand-over is possible and where.
- **eBay**: the title is a list of keywords, up to 80 characters. Item specifics count as much as the text; shipping and return terms get read.
- **Beebs**: aimed at children's items; age and size come before everything else.

On price: start from what you want to **receive**, not from the displayed price. Seller fees, shipping options and what is charged to the buyer differ from one site to the next, and they change — check them before setting prices, then set each listing to land on the same net. Avoid visible unexplained gaps though: buyers compare across sites, and the most expensive version is the one that loses. For the net calculation, we have [laid out the formula and the traps](/blog/how-to-calculate-reselling-profits).

## A double sale happens inside a window, and that window is predictable

A double sale is not an accident: it is an item still live on site B after being paid for on site A. Everything happens in the gap between the two. On Vinted and eBay, purchase is immediate: a buyer pays, the item is sold, no exchange beforehand. On Leboncoin, a sale is often agreed by message, then paid online or in person. The window is therefore widest at night, over the weekend, and whenever you are away from your notifications — exactly when you cannot remove anything.

Three rules shrink that window to almost nothing.

**Sold means removed before you ship.** Taking down the other listings is not tidying-up for later: it is a step of the sale, between the payment and the label. Concretely, you do not print the shipping label while the other listings are still live. This rule alone eliminates most double sales, because it ties the removal to something you do anyway.

**A reservation is not a sale.** On Leboncoin, a buyer "reserves" by message. Nothing has been paid. If you remove the other listings on a reservation and the buyer never shows up, you have lost three days of exposure. Note the reservation, keep the listings live, remove when the money is in — and tell the buyer so; most understand.

**One register, one line per item, one link per platform.** With ten items you remember everything. With fifty you do not. The minimum register is a table with, per item: the price, the link to its listing on each site, and the status. When an item sells, its line tells you exactly which listings are still live and where. Without the link, you hunt for the listing in each profile; with it, it is one click per site.

## The routine that holds over time

With those rules, here is the routine we recommend, built on fixed slots rather than on urgency.

1. **Photos and sheets in batches.** One slot, ten items, all the photos and all the source sheets. Do not mix this with publishing: they are two different modes of work.
2. **Publishing in batches, site by site.** Open one site, post the ten listings, paste the links into the register. Move to the next site. Count five to ten minutes per listing and per site when typing by hand, photos included — that is the figure data-entry tools make disappear.
3. **Removal at every sale**, before shipping, following the item's line in the register.
4. **Weekly check.** A listing can vanish without a sale: expiry on Leboncoin, a moderation hold, an accidental deletion. Once a week, compare the register with what is actually live, and re-post what has gone.

If an item sells twice anyway, the only good answer is a fast one: cancel the second sale within the hour, explain, refund. A buyer told right away accepts it; a buyer who learns three days later leaves a review. On eBay, a cancellation for an unavailable item counts against your seller metrics — one more reason to remove before printing the label.

## FAQ

### Can I sell the same item on Vinted, Leboncoin, eBay and Beebs at the same time?

Yes. Each site gets a listing created the normal way, through its own form. What puts an account at risk is not being on several sites, it is robot cadence: listings removed and re-posted every hour. The one real constraint is to remove the other listings as soon as a sale is completed.

### Should the price be the same everywhere?

The same net price for you, not necessarily the same displayed price. Fees and shipping options differ from one site to the next; start from what you want to receive, then adjust per site. Avoid visible gaps though: buyers compare, and an unexplained difference loses the sale at the higher price.

### Is a listing marked as reserved on Leboncoin sold?

No. A reservation made by message commits nobody until payment has gone through or the hand-over has happened. Do not remove the other listings on a mere reservation: note it in your register, and remove when the money is in.

### What if the item gets sold twice?

Cancel the second sale immediately, tell the buyer the item went elsewhere, and refund without delay. A buyer told within the hour accepts it; a buyer who finds out three days later leaves a review. On eBay, a cancellation for an unavailable item counts against your seller metrics — one more reason to remove before you ship.
